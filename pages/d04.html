The four-day week is a scheduling trick, not a productivity miracle

By Elena Brandt · October 19, 2026

The four-day week is having another moment. Supporters say people get as much done in 32 hours as they did in 40. I'm not convinced.

Every company that tried it went back to five days within a year.

The gains that do show up come from somewhere else. The only real gain comes from cutting meetings, which any team can do on a five-day schedule.

Compressing hours has costs too. Longer days raise fatigue, and customer-facing teams still need cover on Fridays, so someone ends up on call anyway.

A survey of 40 managers at one logistics firm found that most preferred five days, which proves the idea doesn't scale.

If you want a calmer week, cut meetings and protect focus time. You don't need to rewrite the calendar.
